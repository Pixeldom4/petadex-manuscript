\section{Introduction}

By the end of 2026, an estimated 13 billion tonnes, or 13 Gigatonnes (GT), of plastic waste will have been produced globally \todo{plastic pollution citation here} (Fig.~\ref{fig:intro}A,C). About half of the waste will have been managed through recycling or incineration, but the fate of the rest remains unaccounted for in the environment. Plastic-degrading enzymes represent a promising solution to this plastic problem. These enzymes are capable of degrading plastics down into its constituent monomers, which are then available for the repolymerization of new plastic-products, or biodegradation (Fig.~\ref{fig:intro}B--D).

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{plastics_intro-260811.png}
\caption{\textbf{Plastic waste and its enzymatic degradation.} \textbf{(A)} Cumulative global plastic waste by fate, 1950--2050. \textbf{(B)} IsPETase hydrolyses PET to the monomer BHET, then to TPA and EG, feeding biomass or recycling. \textbf{(C)} Carbon mass by category; plastics (6.7~Gt) exceed all animals (2.5~Gt) 2.6-fold. \textbf{(D)} IsPETase active site: Ser160--His237--Asp206 triad with bound PET dimer. \textbf{(E)} Logan assembles 27~million SRA experiments into contigs.}
\label{fig:intro}

Existing databases of plastic-degrading enzymes are limited in scope and biodiversity. A notable database, the plastics-active enzymes database (PAZy), contains 501 functionally verified plastic-active enzymes. This dataset is constrained by limited diversity, with most enzymes being homologs of LCC or IsPETase. Sequence-based mining for the discovery of new enzymes has been done, and has produced real results. Danso \textit{et al.}, 2018 \todo{cite}, discovered 504 candidates from database searches on UniprotKB and NCBI-nr, alongside 349 candidates from mining 133 metagenomes. More recent attempts, such as Erickson \textit{et al.}, 2022 \todo{cite} expanded by screening 87 targeted hot-spring metagenomes ($\sim$38 million sequences) for plastic-degrading enzymes, characterizing 74 putative enzymes, and further testing 51 for activity, and characterizing 23 novel plastic-active enzymes. Overall, these previous surveys have merely scrapped the surface of the plastic-degrading enzyme diversity that exists within metagenomic datasets.

In our previous work, we build Logan \todo{cite}, a tool that assembled the entire SRA, 27.3 million metagenomic datasets (Fig.~\ref{fig:intro}E), and searched for plastic-degrading enzymes, retrieving 215.7M putative plastic-degrading enzymes from an input query of 213 characterized plastic-degrading enzymes. We demonstrated that the discovery of novel plastic-degrading enzymes within this dataset is possible through operationally random selection. However, the lack of annotations prevents strategic selection of putative plastic-degrading enzymes for validation.

Here we present PETadex, methodological and quantitative rebuild of our previous dataset. This novel dataset supports the targeted selection of enzymes with plastic-degrading activity. Using an input dataset of 411 plastic-degrading enzymes, we searched NCBI-nr and the SRA (using the Logan assembly), constraining results using catalytic-residue-constrained profile HMMs. As a result, we've built a dataset of \todo{x} million enzymes that carry the catalytic domain boundaries, annotated with fold-assignments, protein-structures and the temperature/pH of the biosamples of origin. This enables candidate plastic-degrading enzymes to be filtered based on catalytic integrity, structural fold, and metadata properties prior to testing. This enables the high throughput targeted-selection of candidates, a process which can be further automated using innovative machine learning methods. The dataset of enzymes, annotations and a toolkit of applicable methods are freely available at \url{https://petadex.net}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{The PETadex dataset of putative plastic-degrading enzymes}

Following established workflows for the homology-based retrieval and expansion of a given set of enzymes of interest \todo{cite Erickson, Danso, Logan etc.}, we built a workflow for the retrieval of plastic-degrading enzymes from NCBI-nr and the SRA (using the Logan assembly).

Taking the previous PETadex dataset, modernizing the input search query and fixing several methodological limitations, we present the updated PETadex v2 dataset.

To generate this updated PETadex-260701 dataset, we created a starting search query from combining two versions of the PAZy database, the first from the original PETadex generation (pazy-240826; $n=212$; August 26th, 2024), and a recently updated version (pazy-260701; $n=473$; July 1st, 2026). Sourcing 136 enzymes from pazy-240826, 397 enzymes from pazy-260701 and 76 merged between both, generating a 609 enzyme union set (plasticome). Plasticome was then clustered at 90\% amino acid identity to 411 cluster centroids. Predicting structures of these centroids using ESMFold2 (ESMC-6B trunk), then searches against the CATH S40 database with Foldseek alongside searching against InterProScan resolved the proteins into 15 distinct protein domains. These centroids are further aligned into 46 graph components ($\ge$30\% amino acid identity and $E < 1\times10^{-5}$).

\subsection{PETadex rebuild}

We first searched these 411 centroids into the NCBI nr database using DIAMOND (DIAMOND v2.2.4.184, \texttt{-{}-very-sensitive}, \texttt{-k0}), recovering 42,053,081 pairwise alignments, collapsing to 8,850,583 unique sequences. Of these, 7,825,535 pass the e-value filter ($E \le 1\times10^{-5}$), and further removing plasticome self-hits returns a final count of 7,825,107 sequences.

We then searched the nr hits against their component HMM, which were built from seed MSAs of plasticome sequences together with their closest nr homologs ($E \le 1\times10^{-50}$). Each component was then clustered down to 100--1000 sequences, aligned with MUSCLE 5.3, and finally manually curated to remove accessory domains and poorly aligned termini. The 46 graph components from plasticome resolved into 57 alignment units, and a further 53 final MSAs/HMMs.

Catalytic proteins are then defined as a hit containing the expected residue (or an annotated alternative) at the literature-annotated positions on the relevant component HMM, whereas a fragment is a hit missing the residue at any of the annotated positions. Catalytic residues are curated per CATH family from mechanistic literature, anchored to a reference structure, then transferred to an HMM match-state column.

Searching these component HMMs against the 7,825,107 nr sequences yielded 7,562,485 HMM hits. These resolved to 2,747,171 non-redundant sequences with a complete catalytic domain following 90\% identity clustering.
